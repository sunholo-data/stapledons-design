Most of the small stars near home burn cool and red. Their temperate orbits
lie close in, where a planet circles quickly and may keep the same face
towards its sun. A permanent day need not make the whole world unlivable;
an atmosphere or ocean might carry heat into the dark. Whether either
survives is another question.

Young red dwarfs can spend a long time brighter than their settled state.
Their radiation and activity can strip an atmosphere or help a planet lose
water before its present orbit becomes temperate. A quiet red star today
does not tell us the whole history of the world beside it.

Kepler's planet counts give us a reason to look. They do not settle the
climate or the biology. We include these worlds in our search and label
their habitability as debated. Proxima's orbit is the same in every
variation. What we may find there is a different uncertainty.
