# Premise and the loop: the canonical statement

> **Canon.** This document consolidates the game's premise from four sources, so none of it is lost:
> 1. the retired repo's `design_docs/input/game_loop_origin.md` (preserved at [legacy/input/game_loop_origin.md](../legacy/input/game_loop_origin.md));
> 2. the decisions of 2025-12-02 and 2025-12-06 in [design-decisions.md](design-decisions.md);
> 3. [opening-sequence.md](../features/future/opening-sequence.md), [black-holes.md](../features/future/black-holes.md), [spire-mystery.md](../features/future/spire-mystery.md) and [endgame-legacy.md](../features/future/endgame-legacy.md);
> 4. Mark's attended rulings of **2026-10-06**.
>
> Where they disagree, this document and the later date win. Everything marked *tunable* is a parameter to be found by playtesting, not a fixed fact.

## 1. The situation

- **You are the pilot of Earth's only bubble ship.** The Higgs bubble isolates its interior from external mass-energy, so only light, signals and near-massless particles cross. Nobody can replicate it: it formed under extreme conditions on the maiden voyage, and every other attempt collapses.
- **You come back.** The ship emerges violently from the ergosphere of a spinning black hole: the same rogue black hole that is heading for the Solar System. The crew's memories are erased and the ship's AI is confused. You stabilise the tumbling ship amid full SR and GR distortion. Then the ship is **thrown clear, towards Earth**. A Penrose-process slingshot out of the ergosphere is the physics-adjacent reading; *why* the throw points home is part of the spire mystery (§4). You coast in past Saturn and reach Earth.
- **Earth says:** *"Welcome back. We lost contact four years ago. Did the Alpha Centauri mission succeed?"* You remember nothing of it.
- **You bring the news** (Mark, 2026-10-06; this supersedes the retired doc, where Earth told you). Earth doesn't know it is doomed. A dark, non-accreting black hole a few light-years away is very hard to see. Your ship's records of the emergence are the only evidence. **You bring both the news of the doom and its only possible salvation: the one ship that can travel.**
- **Title card:** *You have 100 years. The Earth has N.*

> **The physics of the threat.** A rogue stellar-mass black hole moves at tens to hundreds of km/s. The first isolated one found, OGLE-2011-BLG-0462 (~7 M☉), moves at about 45 km/s. To arrive in N years it must now be close:
>
> | Speed | N = 10,000 yr | N = 24,000 yr |
> |---|---|---|
> | 45 km/s | 1.5 ly | 3.6 ly |
> | 100 km/s | 3.3 ly | 8.0 ly |
> | 300 km/s | 10 ly | 24 ly |
>
> That's in or just beyond the Oort cloud: the opening is a short, real journey home (a few years of Earth time, already spent from the clock). The black hole itself is an **invented scenario object**, the second invented thing after the bubble. It's labelled as such in the star registry (provenance "scenario") and in the Archive, so it's never passed off as a catalogued object. Its mass and speed are tunable along with N.

## 2. The three clocks

| Clock | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ship (the captain's life) | 100 subjective years | The game ends with your death or a mutiny. The bubble society can go on after you. |
| Earth (the hook) | *tunable*: ~10,000–24,000 Earth years until impact, possibly a difficulty setting | the time Earth has. The retired doc said 10,000–100,000, with 24,000 in its opening scene. Mark (2026-10-06): "10k or 24k, whatever we find works best". |
| Epilogue | to Year 1,000,000 | fast-forward to show what the bubble, its legacy and Earth became |

**The Earth clock is a light-year budget.** At cruise, Earth time is at least the distance travelled, so every light-year of path costs at least one year of Earth's remaining time. With ~10,000 years:
- the mission-relevant play area is roughly a 500–3,000 ly sphere around Sol;
- Sgr A* (27,000 ly) is a choice to leave Earth behind.

(See [galaxy-beyond-measurement.md](../features/next/galaxy-beyond-measurement.md).)

## 3. What you are trying to do

**Saving Earth is a goal, not the goal** (2025-12-06), and it is **meant to be very hard** (Mark, 2026-10-06). Paths to it are found across centuries of contact, trade and revisits:

1. Move the Sun (stellar engineering learned from megastructure builders).
2. Move Earth (planetary engines, or shard-tech combined from several civilisations).
3. Counter the black hole (GR technology from civilisations with extreme physics paradigms).
4. Evacuate: a human diaspora by generation ships, arks or substrate export, with the moral arc of an Earth that refuses to leave.
5. The "Sargasso ending": a stable orbit around the black hole.
6. Transcend.

Other valid endings (2025-12-06):
- abandon Earth because galactic life matters more;
- fail but seed a diaspora;
- come back to find Earth solved it without you.

**Earth's fate is always shown at the end**, alongside what the bubble society became.

**What you build changes the odds** (Mark, 2026-10-06): the trade and contact networks the crew creates make a solution more or less likely. Sharing the drive and maps can mean others reach Earth first (an emergent consequence, not a script).

## 4. The loop (the secret)

- **You are not the first.** Earth has been doomed many times. Each failed voyager crossed a black hole into a nearby variation of the universe: the same Solar System, Earth and doom, but slightly different stars, civilisation distributions and anthropic luck.
- **Every run is one of those variations**, and the start of every game is the ship emerging close to the black hole in question.
- **The ship's AI is an unreliable narrator** (Mark, 2026-10-06). It begins confused, and **works it out again in each iteration**: from spire readings, archive errors and patterns in the sky. It may know more than it says, and it may be wrong. (This replaces the open question "Can the Archive ever know its origin?": yes, each time, gradually, never certainly.)
- **The spire ends the run** (Mark, 2026-10-06). One of the spire's deepest mysteries: **once Earth's fate is sealed**, whether saved, lost or settled, the spire resets the bubble and pulls it back to the start of a new variation. It emerges at the rogue black hole again and is thrown towards Earth again. The run doesn't end with an arbitrary game-over; it ends with the spire taking you back. What counts as "sealed" is a design question (§9).
- **The bubble may itself be a pocket universe,** the thing that survives the crossing. This stays deliberately hand-wavy. The bubble is the one invented thing, and the Archive says so.

## 5. Selection: why this universe is so full of life

- **Anthropic selection is the point, and the game shows it** (Mark, 2026-10-06). Measured star counts with realistic Drake values predict about **0.05** civilisations within reach before impact (see [life-and-intelligence-parameters.md](../features/next/life-and-intelligence-parameters.md)). If the player meets dozens or hundreds nearby, that is a statement in itself. This neighbourhood breaks the cosmological principle: the universe is not typical here, because it was *chosen*.
- **Who chooses** (both, by design):
  - **The AI steers** each reset towards variations where success is more possible.
  - **The player's choices** in the previous run make the next variation more or less likely to be winnable.

  The exact mechanism stays mysterious ("clearly beneficial, not explained": open question of 2025-12, still open in detail).
- **The luck number is a diegetic reveal.** It's hidden at world generation, revealed by the AI as part of working the loop out ("this universe is a thousand times kinder to minds than chance would give; that is not an accident"), and visible on New Game+.

## 6. What carries over between runs

| Carries over | How | Diegetic reading |
|---|---|---|
| Measured reality: real stars, real exoplanets, physics | always the same canon (living data updates between game versions, never mid-run) | the same sky |
| The universe-selection weights | the AI's steering plus the previous run's outcome | luck that feels earned, unexplained |
| **Generated content** (Mark, 2026-10-06): assets created on the fly during play (civilisation designs, portraits, voices, art, world textures, lore) | a cross-run content library: content generated in one universe is available to the next, as variations, not verbatim | **déjà vu**: "I have seen this species before." The crew don't remember; the universe does. |
| Memories | **never** | the crossing erases them (2025-12-02) |

This replaces the old constraint "no information survives the BH crossing (that's the point)" with: **no memory survives; the universe's furniture can.** The furniture becomes a clue channel for the recursion and supports replayability.

## 7. Replayability

Many runs are expected. Each is a different variation: different synthetic stars beyond the measured core, different civilisation distributions, different luck. Across runs the player (and the AI) assemble the knowledge of which technologies and philosophies, combined, might save Earth. Saving it is the rare, hard ending that runs build towards.

## 8. Tunable parameters (to be found by playtesting)

- The Earth clock N (10,000 / 24,000 / difficulty-scaled).
- The selection strength: the range of anthropic luck the loop can reach, and how fast steering converges.
- Encounter targets inside the doom window.
- How strongly the previous run's choices weight the next variation.
- How much generated content carries over, and how much it varies.

## 9. Still open

- **Resolved 2026-10-06: the player brings the news** (§1). Earth learns of the doom from you.
- **What seals Earth's fate**, triggering the spire's reset? Candidates: impact; a salvation completed (Sun or Earth moved, evacuation finished, black hole countered); or an irreversible failure. And how does that combine with the captain's 100-year life? If the captain dies before the fate is sealed, does the bubble society play on, or the epilogue run, until the spire resets?
- The black hole's tunable mass and speed (with N), and how the opening flight from it to Earth is staged.
- Universe-hopper encounters (another loop's voyager): rarity unchanged from open-questions.md.
- The meta-save: exactly which data persists, and where (the content library is the first concrete answer).
